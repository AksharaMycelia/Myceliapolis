One seed, two histories

Plant a bit X∈{0,1}X\in\{0,1\}. For any observation OO, compare the two possible plantings. The reconstruction criterion also applies to a rare seed. For the error probabilities below, give the two plantings equal prior weight.

PxO=ℒ(O∣X=x),Δ(O)=12∑o|P1O(o)−P0O(o)|,e*(O)=12(1−Δ(O)). P_x^O=\mathcal L(O\mid X=x),\qquad \Delta(O)=\tfrac12\sum_o|P_1^O(o)-P_0^O(o)|,\qquad e^*(O)=\tfrac12(1-\Delta(O)).

A distant room retains a trace if liminfnΔ(On)>0\liminf_n\Delta(O_n)>0. A reader recovers the planting with vanishing error if Δ(On)→1\Delta(O_n)\to1. Both the room and the reader's ledger will matter.

Flip with probability η∈[0,1/2]\eta\in[0,1/2], then reset to zero with probability β∈[0,1]\beta\in[0,1]. Use independent randomness at each relay. Write uu for the false-positive probability and vv for the false-negative probability.

u=(1−β)η,v=β+(1−β)η,T=(1−uuv1−v),θ=1−u−v=(1−β)(1−2η),Pr(Y′=1∣Y=y)=u+θy,Δ(Yn)=θn(Y0=X). \begin{gathered} u=(1-\beta)\eta,\qquad v=\beta+(1-\beta)\eta,\\ T=\begin{pmatrix}1-u&u\\ v&1-v\end{pmatrix}, \qquad \theta=1-u-v=(1-\beta)(1-2\eta),\\ \Pr(Y'=1\mid Y=y)=u+\theta y,\qquad \Delta(Y_n)=\theta^n\quad(Y_0=X). \end{gathered}

The forest

Each vertex has b≥2b\ge2 children; each edge uses TT independently. Read all bits in generation nn, denoted by LnL_n. On the one-sided boundary η=0\eta=0, zero never gives birth to one. For a living vertex, the number of living children has distribution Binomial(b,s)\mathrm{Binomial}(b,s) with s=1−βs=1-\beta. Starting from one, write qnq_n for the probability of an empty generation of living spores.

q0=0,qn+1=f(qn),f(z)=(1−s+sz)b,qn↑q*=min{q∈[0,1]:f(q)=q},Δ(Ln)=1−qn,f′(1)=bs,limn→∞Δ(Ln)>0⇔bs>1. \begin{gathered} q_0=0,\qquad q_{n+1}=f(q_n),\qquad f(z)=(1-s+sz)^b,\\ q_n\uparrow q_*=\min\{q\in[0,1]:f(q)=q\},\qquad \Delta(L_n)=1-q_n,\\ f'(1)=bs,\qquad \boxed{\lim_{n\to\infty}\Delta(L_n)>0\iff bs>1.} \end{gathered}

One living descendant identifies its ancestor. For two children and s=3/5s=3/5, the extinction equation has roots 4/94/9 and 11.

bs2=1825<1,q*=49,limn→∞Δ(Ln)=59. bs^2=\tfrac{18}{25}<1,\qquad q_*=\tfrac49,\qquad \lim_{n\to\infty}\Delta(L_n)=\tfrac59.

When spontaneous ones can appear, a lone descendant loses that power. For an independent binary broadcast tree the Kesten–Stigum condition bθ2>1b\theta^2>1 suffices for reconstruction. It is exact for symmetric channels and for sufficiently small asymmetry. Some more asymmetric channels permit reconstruction below it. Borgs, Chayes, Mossel & Roch · Mossel

The clay jar

Allow arbitrary gates to merge notes. Give each layer at most WW one-bit outputs, each independently passed through TT after its gate. With θ<1\theta<1, each such channel has a copy-or-refresh realization. With probability θ\theta copy the gate output; otherwise draw a fresh bit from Bernoulli(u/(1−θ))\mathrm{Bernoulli}(u/(1-\theta)). Couple two histories by giving them the same refreshes and fresh draws.

If edges cross at most LL layers, LL consecutive all-refresh layers erase every path from the planting. In any disjoint block of LL layers this event has probability at least aa. Writing OmLO_{mL} for the final layer gives

a=(1−θ)WL,Δ(OmL)≤(1−a)m. a=(1-\theta)^{WL},\qquad \boxed{\Delta(O_{mL})\le(1-a)^m.}

For 0<a<10<a<1 the first sampled depth at which the gap is at most half consequently satisfies

t1/2≤L⌈log2−log(1−a)⌉. t_{1/2}\le L\left\lceil\frac{\log 2}{-\log(1-a)}\right\rceil .

This is an upper bound on the jar's memory. A jar that copies only its newest bit already has gap θn\theta^n at depth nn. A witness who can revisit the planting has an edge extending beyond the jar.

Three relays, two kinds of witness

At each handoff independently, a dog speaks with probability ρ\rho. Otherwise the relay uses TT. Only the resulting bit is visible in the last room. Set dn=Pr(Yn=1∣X=1)−Pr(Yn=1∣X=0)d_n=\Pr(Y_n=1\mid X=1)-\Pr(Y_n=1\mid X=0) and d0=1d_0=1. The bias of a witness reading is c=1−2ϵc=1-2\epsilon, with error probability 0≤ϵ<1/20\le\epsilon<1/2.

dnsilence=θn,dnalwaysone=[(1−ρ)θ]n,dn+1witness=ρc+(1−ρ)θdnwitness. \begin{aligned} d_n^{\mathrm{silence}}&=\theta^n,\\ d_n^{\mathrm{always\,one}}&=[(1-\rho)\theta]^n,\\ d_{n+1}^{\mathrm{witness}}&=\rho c+(1-\rho)\theta d_n^{\mathrm{witness}}. \end{aligned}

The unconditional density of ones may rise even while the distinction between plantings fades. For the witness, Δ(Yn)=dnwitness\Delta(Y_n)=d_n^{\mathrm{witness}} and, for θ<1\theta<1,

d∞=ρc1−(1−ρ)θ,ρmin(δ)=δ(1−θ)c−δθ(0<δ≤c). \begin{gathered} d_\infty=\frac{\rho c}{1-(1-\rho)\theta},\\ \boxed{\rho_{\min}(\delta)=\frac{\delta(1-\theta)}{c-\delta\theta}} \qquad (0<\delta\le c). \end{gathered}

The infimum of rates preserving any positive trace is zero. Every ρ>0\rho>0 preserves some trace. A target above cc is unattainable in the limiting single room.

Now listen to mm witness barks. One dog takes a single reading and copies it faithfully forever. Another dog revisits the planting and makes an independent reading at each bark. They give the same single-room recursion. For odd mm, their complete bark records have different optimal errors:

A dot beside each bark

Use signs x,y∈{−1,+1}x,y\in\{-1,+1\}. Room nn ordinarily emits a fair coin. Independently it receives a fresh witness reading with probability ρn\rho_n and emits that reading instead. The mark BnB_n records which event occurred. Given the root, the rooms are independent.

Bn∼Bernoulli(ρn),Bn⊥X,Pr(Yn=y∣X=x,Bn=0)=12,Pr(Yn=y∣X=x,Bn=1)=12(1+cxy),Pr(Yn=y∣X=x)=12(1+cρnxy). \begin{gathered} B_n\sim\mathrm{Bernoulli}(\rho_n),\qquad B_n\perp X,\\ \Pr(Y_n=y\mid X=x,B_n=0)=\tfrac12,\\ \Pr(Y_n=y\mid X=x,B_n=1)=\tfrac12(1+cxy),\\ \Pr(Y_n=y\mid X=x)=\tfrac12(1+c\rho_n xy). \end{gathered}

For two conditional laws P,QP,Q, their Hellinger affinity multiplies over independent entries. The Bayes error obeys

A(P,Q)=∑zP(z)Q(z),1−1−A22≤e*≤A2. A(P,Q)=\sum_z\sqrt{P(z)Q(z)},\qquad \frac{1-\sqrt{1-A^2}}2\le e^*\le\frac A2 .

The first inequality follows by applying Cauchy–Schwarz to |P−Q|=|P−Q|(P+Q)|P-Q|=|\sqrt P-\sqrt Q|(\sqrt P+\sqrt Q). The second follows from min(P,Q)≤PQ\min(P,Q)\le\sqrt{PQ}. Keeping or erasing the mark gives the two finite-ledger products

Ammarked=∏n=1m[1−ρn(1−1−c2)],Ambare=∏n=1m1−c2ρn2. \begin{aligned} A_m^{\mathrm{marked}} &=\prod_{n=1}^m\left[1-\rho_n\bigl(1-\sqrt{1-c^2}\bigr)\right],\\ A_m^{\mathrm{bare}} &=\prod_{n=1}^m\sqrt{1-c^2\rho_n^2}. \end{aligned}

For fixed 0<c≤10<c\le1 with cρn<1c\rho_n<1 at every entry, taking logarithms gives the exact thresholds:

emmarked→0⇔∑nρn=∞,embare→0⇔∑nρn2=∞. \boxed{\begin{aligned} e_m^{\mathrm{marked}}\to0 &\iff \sum_n\rho_n=\infty,\\ e_m^{\mathrm{bare}}\to0 &\iff \sum_n\rho_n^2=\infty. \end{aligned}}

If c=ρj=1c=\rho_j=1 for even one known index jj, that entry itself identifies the root in either ledger.
